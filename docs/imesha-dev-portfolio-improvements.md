# imesha.dev — Portfolio Improvement Guide
**Goal:** Attract US, UK, Australian & Sri Lankan freelance clients  
**Prepared for:** Imesha Dilshani | imesha.dev

---

## 🔍 Current Site Audit

### What's There Now
The current site positions you as:
> *"Data Engineer, Software Engineer, Data Scientist, and Self-Learner building intelligent software and data systems"*

Alongside the tech work, the site also mentions stock photography, book writing, a reading list, and math classes.

### Core Problem
The site reads like a **personal blog + hobby showcase + portfolio** all mixed together. A client from the US, UK, or Australia visiting to hire you will see "kids books" and "math classes" next to your dev work and immediately question your professional focus.

---

## 🚨 What Needs to Change

### 1. Too Many Hats — Clients Get Confused
Stock photography, book writing, a reading list, math classes — these have no place on a freelancing portfolio. They signal that software development isn't your primary focus.

**Fix:** Move all personal hobby content to a separate `/personal` page or remove it from the main portfolio entirely. Keep the main site 100% professional.

---

### 2. No Clear "Hire Me" CTA
There is no obvious button or section telling a visitor that you are available for freelance work. A client shouldn't have to read three paragraphs to figure that out.

**Fix:** Add a bold, visible CTA in the hero section:
```
[ Available for Freelance Work → Book a Free Call ]
```
Link it to a Calendly or similar scheduling tool.

---

### 3. Headline Is Too Scattered
Listing three job titles at once ("Data Engineer, Software Engineer, Data Scientist") tells a client nothing specific. When you are everything, you are nothing to someone with a specific need.

**Fix:** Replace with a single benefit-focused headline. Example:

> *"I build fast, clean web apps and data systems for startups and local businesses — from Sri Lanka to the world."*

Or a shorter version:
> *"Full-Stack & Data Engineer. I turn messy problems into clean, working software."*

---

### 4. No Services Section
A client cannot tell what you actually offer. Web development? Mobile apps? Data pipelines? Machine learning? Without a clear services section, they will leave rather than ask.

**Fix:** Add a dedicated **Services** section with 3–4 defined offerings. Example structure:

| Service | What's Included | Starting From |
|---|---|---|
| Web App Development | React / Astro / Next.js, Cloudflare deployment, responsive design | $300 |
| Mobile App (React Native) | iOS & Android, Expo, app store deployment | $500 |
| Data Engineering | ETL pipelines, dashboards, database design, automation | $250 |
| AI / ML Integration | Python, data science, model integration into existing apps | $400 |

*(Adjust pricing to your comfort — even a "Starting from" range removes friction)*

---

### 5. No Social Proof
No client testimonials, no project results with numbers, no case studies. For US/UK/AU clients who are hiring across time zones, trust signals are everything.

**Fix:** Add at minimum:
- 2–3 short client testimonials (even from local clients)
- Project cards with a **Problem → Solution → Result** format
- Any measurable outcomes (e.g. "Reduced report generation time by 40%", "Launched in 3 weeks")

---

### 6. "Messy Parts of Tech" Framing
The current bio line *"I enjoy the messy parts of tech"* sounds cool to developers but reads as a red flag to clients, who want reliability and clean outcomes.

**Fix:** Reframe around outcomes and dependability. Example:
> *"I turn complex, tangled problems into clean, maintainable systems — and I actually enjoy doing it."*

---

## 💡 New Sections to Add

### For US / UK / Australian Clients

**A. "Working Across Time Zones" blurb**
A short paragraph reassuring international clients about your communication style. Example:
> *"Based in Sri Lanka (UTC+5:30), I work asynchronously across US, UK, and Australian time zones. You'll get daily updates, fast responses, and clean deliverables — without micromanaging."*

**B. Case Study Cards (2–3 projects)**
Each card should follow this format:
```
Project Name
Client / Industry
Problem: [What the client needed]
Solution: [What you built]
Result: [Outcome / metric / launch]
Tech Stack: [icons]
[ View Project → ]
```
You already have strong candidates: **VeloLanka** (car rental platform), **Wirity** (startup work), **Ranil's OOAK** (ceramic art studio site), and the **IBMS** enterprise system.

**C. "Book a Free 30-Min Call" button**
Link to Calendly or Cal.com. International clients expect this. It removes the barrier of writing a cold email.

**D. Tech Stack Visual Section**
Show your stack as logo icons (React, Python, React Native, Astro, Laravel, MySQL, etc.) so clients can immediately pattern-match your skills without reading paragraphs.

### For Sri Lankan Local Businesses

Add a short intro in Sinhala alongside the English version. Even a single line builds instant trust with local clients:

**B. "Working with Sri Lankan Businesses" Mini-Section**
- Mention you understand local context and business culture
- Note LKR-based pricing availability
- Mention local payment options (PayHere, bank transfer)
- Highlight Western Province / Colombo availability for in-person meetings if needed

**C. Local Project Examples**
Feature at least one clearly Sri Lankan project with context. Local business clients trust people who've worked locally before.

---

### General Improvements

**A. Restructure the Navigation**
Suggested nav structure:
```
Home | Services | Work | About | Blog | Contact
```
Remove: Reads, personal sections from main nav.

**B. Add a Blog / Content Section Preview**
Pull in your latest YouTube video and Medium post as a preview section. This builds authority and shows you're active in the tech community.

**C. Add a "Currently Available" / "Booked Until" Status Badge**
A small badge in the hero like `🟢 Available for projects — June 2026` creates urgency and transparency.

**D. Add a Simple Contact Form**
With fields: Name, Email, Project Type (dropdown), Brief Description, Budget Range. Makes it very easy for clients to reach out without writing a full email.

**E. Footer with All Links**
Make sure the footer includes: email (work@imesha.dev), LinkedIn, GitHub, YouTube, Fiverr link, and location (Sri Lanka — Available Worldwide).
